### What the g=3 +80 actually was (re-measured here, independently)

Re-running the identical instrument on the g=3 phase [2852510, 11329301] reproduces the +80 and locates it precisely — it is **in rung 11's exit span, not in the gap**:

  mid-transition** — it needs 80 more steps to reach the cascadeReg 11 shape. Same start, same end shape,
  **80 extra transit steps.** This is genuine dynamics (a far-field / top-of-ladder boundary effect at g=3),
  not a detector artifact.
- The task computed gap(11) with the *predicted* landing: s_12 − (s_11 + exitSteps(11)) = 9206363 − 5018116 =
  **4188247 = law + 80** → the reported "+80 in gap(11)". Measured from the *observed* cascadeReg 11 the gap is

- At g=4, rung 11's exit span is exitSteps(11) exactly (0 excess), and so is rung 12's (the g=4 rung one-below-top).
  So the +80 is neither "always k=11" nor "always one-below-top"; it is specific to (g=3, k=11) and vanishes at g=4.

**Bottom line for the roadmap: the doubling-phase law (exitSteps(k) transport + gap 4^k−3·2^k+7 between rungs)
